The new school year

$10 covers one child's list.

Sri Lanka gives children free schooling and free textbooks. It doesn't give them the exercise books, the pens, or the pencils — and that list lands on a family in a single month, all at once.

Send a child's supplies

What $10 buys

The list she's expected to walk in with.

  • Exercise books for every subject
  • Pens and pencils
  • Erasers, sharpeners and rulers
  • The rest of the classroom list

What it doesn't buy: a backpack. A backpack alone costs $7 — most of this gift for one item. We put the money into what goes inside one instead. It's a less charming photo and a better use of your $10.

The impact

One list. Two problems solved.

For the child

  • She walks in on day one with the same kit as everyone else.
  • She writes in her own exercise book — no borrowing, no sharing, no falling behind because she couldn't copy the board in time.
  • She isn't kept home over a missing pencil, and isn't the child asked about it in front of the class.
  • She starts the year as a serious student in her teacher's eyes, which quietly shapes how she's treated for the rest of it.

For her family

  • The year's sharpest expense stops landing in a single month.
  • No loan at interest, no pawn shop, no deciding which child gets supplies and which one waits.
  • What isn't spent on the list goes back into food.
  • Less pressure to pull an older child out to work to cover a younger one's list.

Be that one

$10 is a small thing that decides how a whole year starts.
