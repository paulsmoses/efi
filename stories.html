Stories

The reason we started was people, not a plan.

Individual stories of devastation followed by resourcefulness, and despair followed by restoration. Here are a few of them.

INSPIRE · Hambantota

Thushari

Thushari worked as a field officer for an NGO in Colombo, specialising in child development, until she lost her job in 2018. Her husband was let go from his post at a leading hotel in Hambantota because of his epilepsy. Two incomes gone, and no obvious way back.

She taught herself to sew. He started drawing. We funded their places at a batik workshop, run with our local partner We'RE SL, and they began making batik products together. They're now working toward a shop of their own.

This is what micro-business support actually looks like: not a rescue, but the cost of a workshop place at the moment two skilled people needed one.

INSPIRE · Hambantota

Sheni

Sheni attended the same batik workshops and decided to build something from her own backyard. She is one of four sisters, and she has taken on the work of building the family home.

She runs tie-dye and batik projects while studying for a diploma in English. She'd like a sewing machine of her own one day, and after that, a shop.

In Hambantota, most work is labour — salterns, paddy, fisheries — and women aren't much encouraged into economic activity. Girls facing hardship at home often marry in their teens to escape it. Sheni chose a different route, and needed only for someone to fund the first door.

LEAD · North Western Province

The children the teachers wrote down.

Our village teachers keep notes on what changes. These are theirs, not ours.

A girl who went back up a grade

She had been demoted to grade 2 because she couldn't keep up with the work. After attending LEAD classes she was promoted back to grade 3 — the clearest single measure of what an afternoon of help can do.

Shanilka

A grade 11 student who can now work through O-level past papers on his own. The exams that decide a Sri Lankan child's future are the ones he's now equipped to sit.

Hoshadi

A student from Badalgama whose home life makes attendance hard. She progresses slowly, and she keeps coming. Not every story is a turnaround; some are just persistence, and those count too.

More than a hundred children from the three classes and the surrounding area came to the Children's Day programme at Mahakandura — games, refreshments, and a day that was simply theirs.

In honour of

Rev. Dr. David Ephraim

The Foundation carries the name of Johanna's father, who planted churches and a church leadership organisation in Sri Lanka and spent his life serving others. His passion for people in need is why this exists at all.

It's also why the model is what it is. He didn't build institutions from a distance; he worked where he was. So do we.
